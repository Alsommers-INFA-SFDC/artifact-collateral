1 Packaging Hierarchy: Pallet → Case → Consumer Unit (SKU)

▼

Every consumer packaged good exists simultaneously at three physical levels: the Consumer Unit (SKU) that sits on a retail shelf, the Case that groups SKUs for warehouse stocking and DSD delivery, and the Pallet that structures cases for truckload shipping and DC receiving. Each level is a distinct master data entity with its own GTIN, physical dimensions, weight, and compliance obligations. Collapsing these into a single flat product record — which most ERP and PIM systems do — is the root cause of routing failures, receiving errors, wasted space, and compliance gaps.

Why three entities? A retailer's WMS needs the pallet height to plan warehouse slot assignments. The carrier's TMS needs the case weight to calculate load capacity. The store's POS system needs the consumer unit GTIN to ring up a sale. These are three different systems consuming three different sets of attributes — all attached to the same physical product, but at different levels of packaging.

Entity Definitions
📦

Pallet / Shipping Unit

The logistics unit of measure for truckload and LTL freight. Pallets are what carriers price, what DCs receive, and what slot management systems assign to pick locations. Ti × Hi (tier × height) is the most critical pallet attribute — it determines how many cases fit in the pallet configuration and whether a full pallet fits in a standard 53-ft trailer bay or a DC slot.

Primary Consumers
  • Transportation Management System (TMS)
  • Warehouse Management System (WMS) — slot planning
  • DC receiving / labor management
  • EPR compliance reporting (packaging weight by tier)
  • Load optimization & trailer cube planning
📚

Case / Shipper

The stocking and replenishment unit for warehouse pick faces and DSD delivery. Most retailer ordering systems work in case multiples. The case cube drives warehouse slotting efficiency — a mismatch between the case dimensions in MDM and physical reality causes mis-picks, non-conforming shelf allocations, and DSD receiving disputes. Inner pack configuration is critical for display floor setups.

Primary Consumers
  • WMS — pick-face slotting & replenishment
  • Retail ordering system (cases per order)
  • DSD route planning & delivery manifests
  • Receiving / scan-verify at store backroom
  • EPR material weight data for corrugated
📱

Consumer Unit (SKU)

The retail-sellable unit identified by a GTIN / UPC barcode. The consumer unit is what the POS system scans, what the eCommerce catalog displays, and what the consumer takes home. It carries the regulatory compliance data that is visible to the end consumer: nutrition, allergens, recyclability labeling, and country of origin. It is the base entity from which all packaging levels derive their identity.

Primary Consumers
  • Point of Sale (POS) — GTIN scan at checkout
  • eCommerce product catalog & feeds
  • Category management & planogram systems
  • Consumer-facing label compliance (FDA, EU)
  • Retail Media targeting & Google Product feeds
The "Ti × Hi" problem in CPG: Ti × Hi is one of the most commonly wrong attributes in distributor and retailer product data. A brand ships 8×5 (8 cases per layer, 5 layers high = 40 cases per pallet). The WMS has it recorded as 10×4. The result: the DC slots 10 pallets worth of space for a shipment that actually arrives as 12.5 pallets, causing a receiving backup, a carrier detention charge, and a potential chargeback. Accurate pallet data in MDM — validated at onboarding — eliminates this class of error entirely.

2 Product → Item Inheritance & Category Specification Management

▼

In a well-structured product MDM, Product is the logical parent entity representing what a brand makes — a product line, formulation, or commercial offering. Item (SKU) is the physical child entity representing a specific sellable configuration — a size, flavor, format, or packaging variant. The Product-to-Item relationship is the mechanism through which category specifications, regulatory attributes, and shared metadata cascade down to all items in a product family without repeating the data on every SKU record.

⚙️ Attribute Propagation Examples
Category Specification Cascade

Brand registers a new organic granola bar product. At the Product level, the category manager assigns GPC Brick 10000260 — Cereal & Granola Bars. The MDM system automatically applies the 23 GPC brick attributes for that category (base type, flavoring, organic claim, allergen codes, certifications) to every Item (SKU) in the product family — without the data steward touching each variant individually. When the brand reformulates and updates the organic certification at the Product level, all child Items reflect the change.

Override at Item Level

The product family has 4 SKU variants: Original, Dark Chocolate, Nut-Free, and Gluten-Free. The Nut-Free and Gluten-Free variants override the inherited allergen attributes at the Item level, carrying different values than the product-level default. All other attributes (brand, GPC brick, base ingredient list template) remain inherited. MDM enforces that item-level overrides are flagged as exceptions and reviewed during the next product family governance cycle.

Case Count Changes Without Breaking Lineage

A brand reconfigures a case from 12-pack to 8-pack for a new club store format. A new Case entity is created with the new GTIN-14, count, and dimensions — linked to the same parent Item (SKU). The product-level category specification, brand, and regulatory attributes are inherited by both case configurations. The TMS and WMS receive updated case dimension data without any category or compliance data being re-entered.

Pallet Reconfiguration for New DC

A brand is onboarding a new DC partner that requires a different Ti×Hi configuration (standard 8×5 → club store 6×6). A new Pallet entity is created for the club-store configuration, linked to the same Case. The hazmat classification, regulatory category, and brand identity all inherit from the product family. Only the pallet physical attributes change. The EPR material weight is updated to reflect the difference in pallet-level corrugated usage.

Specification Management at Scale: A mid-tier CPG brand with 400 SKUs across 8 product families can manage category specification changes for an entire product line by editing a single Product record. Without Product-to-Item inheritance, the same change requires touching 400 individual item records — introducing inconsistency, stewardship backlog, and compliance exposure. The inheritance model is not optional for brands managing regulatory change at scale.

5 Packaging Regulations & Compliance Requirements

▼

Consumer goods packaging is now subject to a rapidly expanding set of national and regional regulations targeting waste reduction, material disclosure, recyclability labeling, and producer fee obligations. Every regulation in this space requires the same foundational data: packaging material type, material weight per packaging level, and recyclability classification — the exact attributes captured in the SKU, Case, and Pallet entities in the MDM hierarchy. Without accurate master data at each level, brands face regulatory exposure, failed EPR fee calculations, and potential market access restrictions.

🇪🇺 EU Regulations — EPR & PPWR
EU Packaging & Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, 2025+): The PPWR replaces the 1994 Packaging Directive and introduces mandatory recyclability categories, labeling requirements, minimum recycled content thresholds, and per-material EPR fee calculation at the packaging level — not the product level. Brands must report packaging weight by material type, by packaging level (consumer unit, secondary, tertiary). This is exactly the data structure in the Pallet → Case → SKU hierarchy.

🚚 Routing Optimization: How Packaging Data Drives Freight Efficiency
Load Optimization (TMS)

A carrier's TMS uses pallet dimensions and gross weight to calculate the maximum number of pallets per trailer without exceeding axle weight limits. When the TMS has correct Ti×Hi and pallet height, the system can automatically mix loads across multiple brands to achieve 95%+ trailer cube utilization. When pallet data is wrong, the system defaults to conservative estimates — often leaving 15–20% of trailer space empty on every load, paying for air.

DSD Route Profitability

In Direct Store Delivery, a route driver's truck carries cases from a regional warehouse to 15–25 store stops per day. Route profitability is determined by how many cases per route stop the truck can carry, which is governed by case cube and weight. When MDM has accurate case dimensions, the routing engine can maximize stop density and case count per route without exceeding truck payload. An accurate 12-case box at 0.43 ft³ vs. an incorrectly recorded 0.55 ft³ changes how many cases fit on the truck — and whether the route is profitable.

Multi-Retailer Mixed Pallet for Distributors

Wholesale distributors like UNFI and KeHE mix products from different brands on a single pallet for "less than full pallet" replenishment orders to independent grocery stores. The distributor's WMS uses case cube and case weight from each brand's product spec to calculate how many cases from brand A, B, and C fit on a mixed pallet without exceeding height or weight limits. Brands with inaccurate case data create pick failures, rebuilt pallets, and delayed delivery windows — all of which result in distribution deductions billed back to the brand.

Over-Boxing and Material Over-specification

When a brand's product spec lists a case dimension 15% larger than the actual physical case (a common error from pre-launch spec sheets that were never updated post-production), the DC allocates a pick-face slot sized for the larger case. The result is dead shelf space, lower slot utilization, and a retailer that orders in case quantities calibrated to the wrong cube. The brand ships more packaging material than necessary per unit of product moved. Correcting case dimensions in MDM reduces corrugated usage per delivery route and improves carbon intensity per case shipped.

Shelf Life Data & Food Waste

Inaccurate shelf life at the consumer unit level is one of the largest drivers of preventable food waste in the grocery supply chain. If the shelf life in the retailer's WMS reads 270 days (from an unupdated product spec) but the actual product shelf life is 365 days, the WMS triggers a markdown or disposal flag 95 days before the product should be pulled. Accurate shelf life in MDM — per item, variant, and storage condition — directly reduces unnecessary product write-offs across the distribution network.

📈 Revenue Gains: How Accurate Packaging Data Drives Commercial Performance
Faster Distribution Onboarding

When a brand submits a new item to UNFI or KeHE for distribution, the distributor's item-setup team requires complete case and pallet specifications before they will assign a warehouse slot. Brands that submit complete, accurate packaging data at onboarding get their items in distribution 4–8 weeks faster than brands that submit incomplete specs and go through multiple data-correction cycles. Each week of delayed distribution on a $200K/year item = $3,800 in lost revenue.

Retailer New Item Form Acceptance

Major retail chains (Walmart, Kroger, Target) require complete GS1 product data including all three packaging levels before issuing a purchase order. Brands that maintain their MDM hierarchy — with all three GTINs, dimensions, and weights — can auto-populate these forms via GS1 Sync or 1WorldSync. Brands without MDM spend 2–4 weeks per retailer gathering and verifying spec data manually. At a typical brand with 10 retailer activations per year, this is 20–40 weeks of wasted product team time that competes directly with innovation work.

Planogram Compliance & Shelf Availability

A brand's consumer unit dimensions determine how many facings the planogram assigns on the shelf. When the dimensions in the retailer's planogram system are wrong, the shelf space allocation is wrong — either cutting facings the brand should have (lost sales opportunity) or over-allocating space the brand cannot fill (triggering a slotting review). Accurate consumer unit dimensions in MDM, published to the retailer via GS1, ensure the planogram reflects physical reality and the brand captures its full allocated shelf space.

Retail Media & Google Product Feed Quality

Google Shopping and retail media platforms (Kroger Precision Marketing, Walmart Connect) use product spec data including packaging size, weight, and category to determine ad eligibility, search placement, and cost-per-click bids. Products with complete, accurate spec data at the consumer unit level are classified more precisely, appear in more relevant search results, and have higher conversion rates. Incomplete or inaccurate item data is one of the top three reasons CPG brands experience underperformance in retail media campaigns — and it is entirely a master data problem.

The compounding return: The packaging master data captured to satisfy EPR compliance (material weight per level, recyclability) is the same data that optimizes freight loads (weight, cube), enables DC slotting (case dimensions), speeds retailer onboarding (all three GTINs + specs), and powers retail media feeds (consumer unit attributes). The MDM investment is not siloed in the compliance function — it is a shared data asset that pays back across supply chain, commercial, and sustainability simultaneously. Build it once, use it everywhere.
